The Build Everybody Skips

Everybody's building a 6'6 shooter or a 6'10 lockdown big, and then they get switched onto a downhill guard who lives in the paint and can't be stopped. That's this build. A 6'6 Slashing Playmaker with 90 Playmaking, 88 Finishing, and Hall of Fame badges that punish anybody who plays you soft. One build, day one, no specializing into a corner.

Leading With Playmaking

The 90 Playmaking is the engine, so we start there. This isn't a pass-first point guard that stands around — it's the number that lets you break a defender down off the dribble, create your own shot, and still run an offense as a secondary handle. At 90, your dribble moves chain, your handle holds up under pressure, and you can dish when the help commits. That's the "Playmaker" half of the name, and it's the highest attribute on the build for a reason.

Right behind it: 88 Finishing. This is the "Slashing" half. You are not settling for jumpers when you get downhill — you're going through the chest. 88 lets you finish at the rim, absorb contact, and convert the layups and dunks that softer guards get stripped or blocked on.

Then 84 Shooting. This is the key number. You're a slasher first, but 84 means when they duck under the screen or sag off to stop the drive, you make them pay from three. It keeps the defense honest. Drop this and the whole build collapses into a one-dimensional driver everybody walls off.

The Tradeoff, Said Plainly

Here's where I'm honest with you. Defense is 68. Rebounding is 55. This is not a two-way lockdown, and it's not crashing the glass. You picked offense, and the sheet reflects it. On a competitive Rec team you're the primary scorer and secondary creator — you are not the guy guarding the other team's best perimeter option all night.

What you get back for that is 85 Physicals. That's the speed, the acceleration, and the burst that make the slashing real. A 90 handle with slow legs is useless. 85 Physicals is what turns a dribble move into a blow-by, and it feeds every finish at the rim.

The Badges

Aerial Wizard at Hall of Fame. This is your alley-oop and lob threat — the badge that turns a cut and a lob into an easy two, and makes you a live target every time you're above the break with a passer who can find you.

Downhill at Hall of Fame. This is the identity badge. It rewards you specifically in transition and on the drive to the rim — exactly the situations this build is built to create. Hall of Fame Downhill on 88 Finishing and 85 Physicals is the core of the whole thing.

Deadeye at Gold. This is your answer to a closeout. When the defender flies at you on the catch, Deadeye reduces the penalty for shooting contested — so those catch-and-shoot threes off a kick-out still fall.

Quick First Step at Gold. This one pairs directly with the 90 Playmaking. It sharpens the first move out of a size-up, so when you go, you're already gone.

The Takeovers

Two takeovers, both offensive, both on-theme. Shot Creator boosts your ability to score off the dribble — that's your Playmaking and Shooting getting a live bump when you're cooking. Slasher boosts your finishing and attacking downhill — that's your Finishing and Physicals spiking right when you're already in attack mode. You're not splitting your takeover between offense and defense. You committed. Both of these make the thing you already do better instead of patching a weakness.

The Mechanics It Leans On

This build depends on getting downhill and finishing through contact — that's the 88 Finishing and Hall of Fame Downhill doing the work on drives and in transition. It depends on creating off the dribble, which is the 90 Playmaking and Gold Quick First Step generating separation. It depends on the lob game, which is Hall of Fame Aerial Wizard turning cuts into oops. And it depends on the catch-and-shoot three from a kick-out, which is 84 Shooting and Gold Deadeye keeping that shot alive under a closeout. Every mechanic here feeds the same downhill, self-creating, finish-or-kick style.

The One Loop To Remember

Break your man down with the 90 Playmaking and Quick First Step, get downhill into Slasher takeover where 88 Finishing and Hall of Fame Downhill finish through the contact — and when the help rotates, you either lob it up for the Aerial Wizard oop or kick it out and knock down the Deadeye three yourself.
